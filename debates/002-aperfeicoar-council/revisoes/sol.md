## Comparison and present recommendation

By criterion 1, immutable originals, common closed inputs, dissent and explicit human decision are shared design strengths. No argument establishes that fuller tables, more models or an external synthesizer are intrinsically safer. Argon's broad exemptions currently introduce the largest unsupported conditions. Sonnet's safeguards are useful once their isolation and evaluation claims are narrowed.

By criterion 2, Astra's capture/normalization corrections and the common deterministic assembly proposal are directly specifiable. Structural checks still need known-input fixtures; two identical builds can repeat the same omission. By criteria 3/4, cost rankings remain hypotheses. The current outputs demonstrate substantial text volume, not a measured effect of adopting any proposed simplification.

My preferred direction is one authoritative artifact register, optional deterministic assembly, preserved full originals, concise participant-specific exceptions, and decision-focused objection records. Keep a manually executable equivalent. First compare mechanical correctness; separately evaluate whether reasoning controls improve decisions. Do not change the named baseline arm halfway through that comparison.

Integration conditions: a no-overwrite capture feeds an immutable register; a packet builder consumes the explicit frozen inventory; closure accepts or flags artifacts without silently deleting them; review receives the full closed set; synthesis dispositions preserve original IDs and conditions; human sign-off names the revision it controls. Audit outcomes and structural lint never advance the human approval state automatically.
