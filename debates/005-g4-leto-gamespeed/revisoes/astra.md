## Brief — up to 300 words

I recommend retaining the shared direction: freeze the actual G4 Leto policy and dependencies, measure complete recorded self-play games, then evaluate persistent isolated workers and measured hot-path changes. Normal supplies the clearest initial host alternative and implementation boundaries. Astra adds important state-recovery, policy-comparison and deployment safeguards. Sonnet raises a consequential baseline/provenance discrepancy that must be checked before historical parity is reused. Sol supplies a sound high-level framework but does not satisfy the requested code-specific architecture and concrete ten-level mapping on its own.

The principal disagreement concerns what establishes preserved quality. Sonnet treats matching move lists on a finite corpus as proof of decision identity and an exemption from a strength tolerance. Normal also makes its match-strength gate conditional on changes not demonstrated equivalent. A finite parity suite supports equivalence within that suite; it does not establish general equivalence or discharge R2’s requested paired strength comparison.

Sonnet’s budget-based ladder is a legitimate conditional alternative to the style-first designs, but U3 remains unresolved. Neither monotonically increasing budgets nor deity names establish increasing playing strength.

My preferred advice is therefore the stateful worker architecture, conditionally selected through an actual host comparison, with universal correctness and paired-quality evidence. Keep style and difficulty separate, retain current progress, and require runtime-bound Leto identity. No throughput winner, strength preservation result or functioning personality is established by these proposals.

### 6. Current recommendation and decisive gaps

My advice to Opus is to retain the stateful worker-pool direction from Normal and Astra, with Sol’s conservative full-path accounting and Sonnet’s provenance checks. Treat the runtime choice and optimization order as conditional until the declared target workload is measured.

Three distinctions should govern the comparison:

1. **Baseline identity versus historical validation.** The report/current-manifest discrepancy is reported by Sonnet and Astra, but their repetition does not verify it. Retrieve the exact underlying passages and bind any future result to the actual dependency set.
2. **Implementation equivalence versus quality acceptance.** Parity is valuable regression evidence. It must not become an unsupported general proof or an exemption from R2.
3. **Style progression versus strength progression.** Either can form a coherent campaign. Select the relationship explicitly under U3; preserve neutral Leto and keep intentional difficulty changes out of the primary throughput comparison.

Two potentially decisive gaps deserve examination even though each is developed primarily by one proposal:

- **Sonnet’s native-provenance discrepancy:** if the cited provenance file names different native assets from the Leto manifest, an identity check could validate the wrong reference. Verify actual artifact hashes and determine which metadata is authoritative.
- **Astra’s alternating-color worker behavior:** if switching sides resets the player or shares calibration under a color-insensitive key, adapting the interactive controller directly to self-play could change engine state on every turn. Verify the reset/key logic and test persistent independent White/Black state.

Normal’s compatibility warning also matters: an unmodified browser-oriented loader must not be assumed to work in Node merely because both execute JavaScript.

These are recommended examination priorities, not a final plan or decree.

## Risks and checks

The recommendations should change if:

- Source verification invalidates the alleged bottlenecks or state hazards.
- A candidate improves component timing but not comparable, recorded complete-game throughput.
- A proposed baseline change conceals a quality regression.
- Legal replay or terminal checks disagree, even when candidate and baseline move lists match.
- Paired quality results fail Martim’s chosen criterion or remain inconclusive.
- Personality distinctions require an unaccepted strength loss or cannot be observed.
- Runtime identity cannot be tied to fetched assets and the active worker.
- Migration or rollback hides existing or newly earned progress.
